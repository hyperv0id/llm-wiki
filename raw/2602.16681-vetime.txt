                                                                                            Abstract
                                                    Time-series anomaly detection (TSAD) requires identifying both immediate point
                                                    anomalies and long-range context anomalies. However, existing zero-shot founda-
                                                    tion models face a fundamental trade-off: 1D temporal models provide fine-grained
                                                    pointwise localization but lack a global contextual perspective, while 2D vision-
                                                    based models capture global patterns but suffer from weak temporal alignment
                                                    and coarse-grained localization. To resolve this dilemma, we propose VETime,
                                                    the first zero-shot TSAD framework that unifies temporal and visual modalities
                                                    through fine-grained visual-temporal alignment and dynamic fusion. VETime
                                                    introduces a Traceable Image Conversion and a Patch-level Temporal Alignment
                                                    module to establish a shared visual-temporal timeline, preserving discriminative
                                                    details while maintaining temporal sensitivity. Furthermore, we design an Anomaly
                                                    Window Contrastive Learning mechanism and a Task-adaptive Multi-modal Fusion
                                                    to adaptively integrate the complementary perceptual strengths of both modalities.
                                                    Extensive experiments demonstrate that VETime significantly outperforms state-
                                                    of-the-art models in zero-shot scenarios, achieving superior localization precision
                                                    with lower computational overhead than current vision-based approaches.


                                         1     Introduction
                                         Time-series anomaly detection (TSAD) is a fundamental yet challenging problem, which requires
                                         identifying rare deviations while providing precise temporal localization. In practice, domain
                                         heterogeneity and resource-constrained deployment render dataset-specific training impractical,
                                         driving the development of Time-Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) for zero-shot anomaly detection.
                                         In this setting, models [Shentu et al., 2024, Lan et al., 2025] are evaluated directly on unseen target
                                         domains without any target-specific training or fine-tuning.
                                         A general zero-shot anomaly detector must handle at least two complementary anomaly patterns, as
                                         shown in Figure 1(a). Point anomalies appear as abrupt, short-lived numerical deviations [Shentu
                                         et al., 2024], requiring fine-grained local sensitivity. Context anomalies correspond to longer-range
                                         violations or structural context [He et al., 2026], requiring a macroscopic view of the sequence.
                                         Models that overfit to either local continuity or global structure may generalize poorly across unseen
                                         datasets where anomaly duration and morphology vary drastically.

 A natural progression to resolve this dilemma is integrating temporal and visual modalities to
 leverage their complementary strengths. Although pioneers like Time-VLM [Zhong et al., 2025] and
 Aurora [Wu et al., 2026] explore multi-modal time-series modeling, they are tailored for forecasting.
 This causes critical task misalignment: forecasting prioritizes macro sequence trends over dense
 timestamp-level localization, while prediction-residual paradigms struggle to generalize across
 diverse anomaly patterns under zero-shot settings. Consequently, developing a native zero-shot
 multimodal TSAD framework requires tackling two key obstacles: Visual-Temporal Ordered
 Alignment: restoring explicit temporal indexing onto a shared timeline required for robust anomaly
 discrimination during 1D-to-2D visual conversion and multi-modal alignment; and Dynamic Synergy
 for Diverse Anomaly Patterns: adaptively fusing 1D local correlations and 2D macroscopic patterns
 to provide comprehensive context information for tackling complex unseen anomaly scenarios.
 To bridge these gaps, we propose VETime, the first framework that unifies temporal and visual features
 via effective alignment and fusion, enabling robust anomaly detection and precise localization, as
 shown in Figure 1(c). To overcome the alignment barrier, we first develop a novel Traceable
 Image Conversion method which embeds the 1D sequence into a 2D topological manifold, preserving
 information-dense anomaly details without losing explicit temporal indices. Complementing this, we
 propose the Patch-level Temporal Alignment module, which enhances 2D visual representations from
 the pre-trained ViT with a 1D temporal ordering, establishing the structural basis for fine-grained cross-
 modal interaction. Subsequently, to facilitate dynamic synergy, we introduce two key mechanisms.
 Considering the distinct perceptual characteristics of visual and temporal modalities regarding
 anomalies, we propose a specialized Anomaly Window Contrastive Learning. This incorporates
 intra- and inter-window contrast between multi-modal features to achieve comprehensive anomaly
 identification. Finally, a Task-adaptive Multi-modal Fusion module equipped with a reconstruction
 head is applied to facilitate robust multi-modal fusion. This mechanism performs dynamic, adaptive
 weighted fusion of the aligned features, ensuring precise performance in downstream anomaly
 detection. Our main contributions are listed as follows:

2      Related work
TSAD and time-series foundation models. Traditional TSAD relies on domain-specific recon-
struction or prediction models [Hundman et al., 2018, Su et al., 2019, Audibert et al., 2020, Tuli
et al., 2022, Xu et al., 2022, Yang et al., 2023]. Recently, time-series foundation models (TSFMs)
have advanced cross-domain zero-shot generalization via large-scale pretraining [Goswami et al.,
2024, Das et al., 2024, Ansari et al., 2024, Shi et al., 2024, Ekambaram et al., 2025, Gao et al.,
2024]. Anomaly-oriented models such as DADA and TimeRCD [Shentu et al., 2024, Lan et al., 2025]
further introduce anomaly-specific objectives or synthetic anomaly supervision for zero-shot TSAD.
Despite preserving fine-grained local temporal precision, these 1D numerical approaches inherently
lack the macroscopic perspective required for identifying long-range contextual anomalies. VETime
addresses this gap by complementing zero-shot temporal modeling with traceable 2D visual context
and visual-temporal fusion.

Time-series-to-image representations and vision-enhanced time-series modeling. Transforming
1D time series into 2D visual representations allows models to leverage mature Vision Transformers
(ViTs) and capture multi-scale periodic patterns [He et al., 2026]. Traditional methods often rely on
fixed spatial-temporal transforms like Gramian Angular Fields (GAF) or Markov Transition Fields
(MTF) [Wang and Oates, 2015]. However, these transformations are non-invertible, destroying
temporal phase details essential for point-wise localization. Recent vision-enhanced methods [Chen


                                                                3
et al., 2025, Wang et al., 2024, Dong et al., 2025] dilute the mapping between image patches and
original timestamps, inevitably introducing severe information bottlenecks. In contrast, our work
introduces an adaptive folding mechanism with exact spatial-temporal mapping, maintaining full
reversibility and fine-grained temporal fidelity.

Vision- and VLM-based zero-shot TSAD. Recent studies [Zhuang et al., 2024, He et al., 2026,
Xu et al., 2025] also leverage pretrained vision or multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for
anomaly detection. VLM4TS [He et al., 2026] localizes candidate anomaly intervals via the ViT4TS
module and applies a VLM to verify and refine them using global visual context. While validating the
efficacy of visual perception for global patterns, their reliance on rendered line plots or prompt-based
reasoning incurs high inference costs and weak timestamp-to-patch mapping. In contrast, VETime is
an efficient detector that realigns visual patches to 1D temporal tokens, enabling dense pointwise
anomaly scoring without computationally expensive VLM prompting.

3     Methodology
To harness the differentiated yet complementary advantages of temporal and visual modalities, we
propose a unified framework capable of fine-grained temporal alignment and dynamic interaction for
anomaly detection. As illustrated in Figure 2, the framework comprises four core components:

       • Traceable Image Conversion (TIC): Transforms 1D time series into traceable visual
         representations, encapsulating global and local correlations into semantically rich visual
         formats.
       • Patch-level Temporal Alignment (PTA): Realigns visual features to the temporal axis to
         ensure fine-grained semantic correspondence.
       • Anomaly Window Contrastive Learning (AWCL): Leverages the complementary nature
         of visual and temporal modalities to enforce multi-scale discriminability.
       • Task-adaptive Multi-modal Fusion (TMF): Dynamically integrates multimodal features
         using sequence reconstruction as an auxiliary constraint.

Ultimately, the fused representations capitalize on the synergy between temporal and visual domains,
facilitating accurate localization across diverse anomaly types. It is noted that our framework supports
univariate and multivariate time series settings. The main text focuses on univariate cases, with the
multivariate extension detailed in Appendix A.

3.1   Traceable image conversion

To accentuate anomalous signals, we transform univariate time series into high-density visual repre-
sentations via a three-stage pipeline as shown in Figure 3(a).

Multi-channel intensity mapping: Unlike prior single-channel mapping approaches [Chen et al.,
2025], we employ a multi-channel encoding strategy, which capitalizes on the standard RGB archi-
tecture to incorporate denser information and better expose latent anomalous patterns. Following
DLinear [Zeng et al., 2023], the raw series X ∈ RL is decomposed into a trend component Xtrend
and a remainder component Xrem , where L is the length of the series. The triplet {X, Xtrend , Xrem }
is independently Min-Max normalized to [0, 255] and mapped to the R, G, and B channels, yielding a
1 × L × 3 tensor that explicitly encompasses both global trends and high-frequency residuals.

Adaptive folding: To accommodate diverse temporal scales, we adopt a periodicity-based fold-
ing strategy [Wu et al., 2023] to transform a 1D sequence into a 2D grid with dimensions
(Tf old , ⌈L/Tf old ⌉, 3). √
                           The folding period Tf old is dynamically estimated via the autocorrelation
function (defaulting to L for non-periodic samples), and adjusted to be a multiple of the ViT patch
size to prevent temporal information discontinuity. For indivisible lengths, we use the mean of the
final period to pad trailing segments, minimizing distribution shifts compared to zero-padding.

Dimension-aware scaling: To standardize input resolution from (Tf old , ⌈L/Tf old ⌉, 3) to
(224, 224, 3) without fidelity loss, we apply a decoupled scaling strategy. We use linear interpolation

Anomaly context windows: During source-domain pre-training, we dynamically construct adap-
tive windows around anomalies. Point-level labels are converted to patch-level labels (set to 1 if
any timestamp within the patch is anomalous). For a continuous anomaly segment spanning La
patches, we define an Anomaly Context Window by symmetrically extending the anomaly segment
bidirectionally into the normal context, with a maximum length of Lw = 2La . Each window contains
one continuous anomaly instance surrounded by its local context.

4      Experiments
Datasets and metrics. Our evaluation is conducted on a comprehensive suite of 26 public datasets
from the TSB-AD benchmark [Liu and Paparrizos, 2024], comprising 11 univariate (excluding
versions derived from multivariate sources to prevent evaluation bias [Wu and Keogh, 2022]) and
15 multivariate datasets. We evaluate performance using four standard metrics: Affiliation-F1, F1-
T, Standard-F1, and VUS-PR. Comprehensive details regarding dataset statistics, formal metric
definitions, and baseline configurations are provided in Appendix B.

4.2    Ablation study

Table 4 ablates individual VETime components (see Appendix D for extended analyses on architec-
tures, mapping strategies, and training objectives), revealing two key design imperatives:

Visual-temporal alignment (TIC + PTA). Effective multimodal modeling requires explicit feature
alignment rather than naive visual conversion. TIC generates discriminative visual anomaly repre-
sentations, while PTA remaps these features onto a shared 1D timeline. Removing this foundation
(V6 and V7) causes multimodal interaction to fail, often degrading performance below the unimodal
baseline.

Dynamic aggregation (AWCL + TMF). Merely generating and aligning visual representations
(V1 and V2) yields marginal gains. Substantial improvements emerge when AWCL enforces multi-
scale discriminability, successfully isolating short-term (local) and long-term (global) anomalies.
Concurrently, TMF’s task-adaptive routing dynamically allocates modality importance, significantly
outperforming simple concatenation. This synergy (V3 and V4) enables the full model to resolve
complex anomalies across challenging datasets like NAB and WSD.

4.3    Model analysis

This section explores the model’s internal mechanisms through interpretability and sensitivity evalua-
tions. Complementary analyses covering feature separability, computational overhead, and expanded
visualizations are detailed in Appendices C and E.

Effective receptive field (ERF) analysis: Figure 5 visualizes the ERF of both branches, demon-
strating that the visual branch attends to cross-periodic features, thereby overcoming the intrinsic
locality of 1D receptive fields. For a marked region, the temporal branch is confined to adjacent
segments, while the visual branch attends to long-range features across periods. This stems from
aggregating multi-component interactions (via RGB mapping) and structurally converting long-range
temporal distances into spatial adjacencies (via folding). This restructuring enhances information
routing efficiency and yields semantically dense tokens, providing a global perspective absent in pure
1D models. Further structural analysis is detailed in Appendix C.4.

Hyperparameter sensitivity: We evaluate the impact of λaw , λe , and τ as shown in Figure 6.
For λaw and τ in contrastive learning, deviations from empirical optima degrade performance, as
excessive values over-smooth feature distributions. Additionally, λe is crucial for preventing expert
collapse; insufficient regularization (< 0.2) impairs modality balancing and weakens discriminability.

Visualization of fusion weights. Figure 7 reveals a pronounced task divergence in routing weights:

5         Conclusion and limitations
We propose VETime, which is the first framework to fuse visual and temporal modalities for zero-shot
time-series anomaly detection TimeRCD
                                (TSAD). To bridge the gap between heterogeneous modalities, we
introduce a Traceable Image Conversion method combined with Patch-level Temporal Alignment.
Furthermore, by leveraging Anomaly Window Contrastive Learning and Task-adaptive Multi-modal
Fusion, the model addresses the limitations of unimodal approaches in capturing diverse anomaly
patterns. Extensive experiments validate its state-of-the-art zero-shot performance. Future work will
incorporate textual modalities to provide semantic explanations for detected anomalies.
Limitations. (1) Sensitivity to high-frequency noise: Reliance on seasonal-trend decomposition
makes VETime susceptible to irregular fluctuations where true structure and noise are entangled.
(2) High-dimensional constraints: The channel-independent multivariate extension may miss
complex inter-variable dependencies, and mapping ultra-high-dimensional data to a fixed visual
canvas risks obscuring fine-grained signatures. (3) Overhead and interpretability: Incorporating
a vision backbone inevitably increases computational complexity. Moreover, while our empirical
evaluations are exhaustive and align with prior works, establishing rigorous theoretical justifications
for multi-modal time-series representations remains an inherent, field-wide challenge.

A     Multivariate model adaptations

In the multivariate setting, we adapt the model as follows:
For multivariate time series input, inspired by the proven efficacy of Channel-Independent (CI)
architectures in recent time-series foundation models, we extend the aforementioned univariate
Traceable Image Conversion to construct a unified three-channel heatmap representation. By
treating each variable independently, we avoid the optimization bottlenecks in modeling complex,
noisy cross-channel correlations in zero-shot scenarios. Specifically, each variable is first decomposed
via Seasonal-Trend decomposition, and subsequently transformed through a variable-specific Gamma
correction to apply differentiated non-linear mappings. To strictly preserve the zero-shot constraint,
this Gamma parameter is dynamically computed during inference using only the statistics of the
current input instance, preventing any leakage of target-domain global priors. This strategy enhances
inter-variable discriminability in the visual domain by adapting to their distinct statistical distributions.
To prevent distorted periodicities and signal discontinuities at variable boundaries, the folding period is
calculated prior to concatenation. Specifically, we estimate the period for each variable independently
and select their greatest common divisor as the unified folding period. The resulting individual
heatmaps are then concatenated along the temporal dimension to form an integrated multivariate
heatmap of size 1 × (Nv L) × 3, where Nv denotes the number of variables and L the temporal
length per variable. This composite representation then undergoes the folding and scaling operations.
Crucially, to address the high dimensionality where the vertical axis after folding may far exceed the
target resolution, an adaptive pooling operation is applied to dynamically downsample the vertical
dimension to exactly 224 if it is greater than 224. This resolves the limitations of copy-padding in
multivariate scenarios and yields a consistent and unified visual input.
Regarding Patch-level Temporal Alignment, the folding and scaling operations applied to the image
features remain unchanged in the multivariate setting; the output is F̂V ∈ RNT S ×Nv ×DV . However,
we inject an identical positional encoding vector EP OS into the token sequence of each variable.
Furthermore, we adapt the self-attention mechanism by leveraging the any-variate attention [Woo
et al., 2024], which processes each variable’s temporal dynamics correction independently. Formally,
the output is formulated as FV ∈ R(NT S Nv )×DT S . This design enables the model to separately
reorganize the temporal order of features for each variable.
The Anomaly Window Contrastive Learning objective is applied independently to the feature
sequence of each variable. Specifically, for every variable, we construct its own anomaly context
window and perform positive/negative sample selection within its own temporal trajectory.
Finally, the Task-adaptive Multi-modal Fusion module remains unmodified in the multivariate
setting, preserving its original architecture and functionality.

B.3   Evaluation setting and zero-shot protocol

To ensure a rigorous and transparent evaluation, we explicitly clarify our zero-shot setting as follows:

      1. Definition of Zero-Shot Setting: In this work, “zero-shot” strictly denotes zero target-
         domain training or fine-tuning. Following TSFM pre-training protocols [Shentu et al.,
         2024, Lan et al., 2025], VETime is trained solely with anomaly supervision on a synthetically
         generated corpus (0.5B points) constructed from mathematical equations. This synthetic
         pre-training incorporates zero statistical priors or labels from target benchmark datasets.
      2. Strict Target-Domain Isolation: All model selection, early stopping, and hyperparameter
         configurations (λaw , λe , τ ) are determined exclusively on a held-out validation set split from
         our synthetic corpus. Any dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning on the target evaluation
         domains is strictly prohibited.
      3. Unsupervised Anomaly Thresholding: For metric calculation (e.g., F1 scores), we employ
         the unsupervised Peaks-Over-Threshold (POT) dynamic thresholding algorithm [Siffer et al.,
         2017]. Anomaly thresholds δ are adaptively calculated for each individual time series


                                                   16
         based solely on test-time predicted score distributions, completely without access to target
         benchmark ground-truth labels.
      4. Baseline Evaluation Protocols: Zero-shot baselines are evaluated using their official pre-
         trained weights, as retraining unsupervised reconstruction models on anomaly-inclusive
         synthetic data would invalidate their detection mechanisms.

B.4   Evaluation metric calculations

Our performance evaluation is conducted using four metrics: Affiliation-F1, Temporal-F1 (F 1T ),
Standard-F1 (F 1), and Volume Under Surface - Precision/Recall (VUS-PR).

       • Standard-F1 is a widely used metric that provides a harmonic mean of precision and
         recall. It is calculated using point-wise True Positives (TP), False Positives (FP), and False
         Negatives (FN).
       • F1-T as described by Sarfraz et al. [2024], is a range-based metric that evaluates anomaly
         detection performance by considering the temporal context of anomalies. It is a variant of
         the F1-score that addresses common issues in time series evaluation, such as overlapping
         predictions and temporal proximity.
       • Affiliation-F1 is a distance-based metric that measures the "affiliation" or proximity between
         the ground truth and predicted anomaly points. It is designed to be less sensitive to minor
         temporal shifts in the predicted anomalies. The score is calculated by finding the optimal
         one-to-one mapping between the ground truth and detected anomalies and then computing
         the F1-score based on these affiliations [Huet et al., 2022].
       • VUS-PR is a threshold-independent, parameter-free metric for time series anomaly detection.
         Unlike point-wise metrics, VUS-PR is robust to time lags and measures the area under a 3D
         surface plot of precision, recall, and a buffer parameter [Paparrizos et al., 2022]. It addresses
         the limitations of standard F1 scores by creating a continuous buffer region around each
         anomaly, thus providing a more reliable and nuanced evaluation of model performance.

B.5   Implementation details

Implementation: The proposed model was implemented using the PyTorch framework on Linux
with one H20 24GB GPU. The input time series is divided into patches of size 16. The visual and
temporal backbones employ the encoders of the frozen MAE [Chen et al., 2025] and pre-trained
transformer architecture [Lan et al., 2025], respectively, with feature dimensions of 768 and 512.
These are projected to a shared dimension of 512 during fusion. Hyper-parameters λaw , λe , and τ are
fixed at 0.1, 0.2, and 0.1. The model is trained on synthetic data [Lan et al., 2025] with a batch size
of 32, using AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 5e-4 and a weight decay of 1e-5, for up to 25
epochs, with early stopping if no improvement is observed for 4 consecutive epochs. For extremely
long sequences, VETime follows the sliding-window design of the temporal backbone and processes
the input sequentially with a fixed window size of 5000 rather than compressing the full series into
one image, which improves scalability with respect to sequence length.

Training data: To ensure robust zero-shot generalization, we constructed a large-scale synthetic
dataset comprising a total of 0.5 billion data points following Lan et al. [2025]. The synthetic
generation process relies strictly on predefined mathematical equations and does not incorporate any
target-domain statistical priors, ensuring absolutely zero exposure to any target-domain data. The
dataset features sequences with variable lengths ranging from 500 to 10,000 time steps. It is designed
to cover a diverse spectrum of anomaly patterns, encompassing both long-term context deviations
(e.g., trend shifts) and fine-grained local anomalies (e.g., point outliers).

Encoders: Our framework leverages a dual-encoder architecture to process heterogeneous modali-
ties.

       • Vision Encoder: We adopt the encoder from a pre-trained Masked Autoencoder (MAE) as
         our visual backbone. Specifically, we utilize the lightweight ViT-Base architecture to extract
         robust semantic representations from the converted time-series images.


                                                   17
        • Time-Series Encoder: We employ a classical Transformer architecture consisting of
          learnable positional encodings, a stack of Encoder layers with Multi-Head Self-Attention
          (MHSA), and Feed-Forward Networks (FFN), followed by Layer Normalization. This
          encoder features 8 transformer layers, each with 8 attention heads. We initialize our encoder
          using the pre-trained weights from Time-RCD [Lan et al., 2025].

To achieve parameter-efficient adaptation, we apply Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to fine-tune
the linear projection matrices within both the Attention mechanism and the FFN modules in the
time-series encoder. Specifically, we set the LoRA rank r = 8 and the scaling hyperparameter
α = 16, resulting in a scaling factor of α/r = 2. LoRA adapters are inserted into all trainable linear
layers of the encoder, while the original pre-trained weights remain frozen during fine-tuning.

AWCL window size selection: The AWCL window mechanism is exclusively active to compute
contrastive losses during the synthetic pre-training phase. While its base size is informed by source-
domain labels (Lw = 2La ), the synthetic corpus inherently contains highly diverse anomaly lengths
(La ranging from a few steps to hundreds). Consequently, the absolute physical receptive field of Lw
is constantly shifting. This implicitly acts as scale-jittering, forcing AWCL to learn scale-invariant,
domain-agnostic representations of local (intra) and global (inter) variations rather than overfitting
to a specific curriculum ratio. During inference, the fused feature is simply concatenated from
the bimodal features (FA = FFN([ZT S ; ZV ])) without constructing windows. Therefore, when
deploying to new datasets, there is no need to adjust any window size, ensuring ease of use and full
zero-shot reproducibility.

C.4   Normal-anomaly feature separability

To further assess the discriminative power of the learned representations, we evaluated normal-
anomaly feature separability of 1D temporal (FT S ), 2D visual (FV ), and fused (FAD ) outputs using
the Calinski-Harabasz (CH) Index and Silhouette Coefficient. As shown in Figure 10, FT S excels at
capturing local spikes, while FV trades local precision for stronger global awareness. Crucially, the
fused representation FAD successfully synergizes both, achieving the highest separability across all
scenarios.
The unique advantages of the 2D visual branch stem from three key mechanisms:

       • Cross-Periodic Dependencies via folding: Following the 2D temporal modeling principles
         established in TimesNet [Wu et al., 2023], the folding mechanism converts these long-
         range temporal distances into vertical spatial adjacencies. This enables the 2D backbone
         to efficiently gain cross-periodic patterns without requiring inefficiently large 1D receptive
         fields.
       • Cross-Component Interaction via Channel Mapping: By mapping the raw signal, Trend,
         and Remainder components into distinct RGB channels, ViT integrates the complex interplay
         between trend and residual information into unified token embeddings, naturally achieving
         early cross-component fusion.

Effectiveness of Traceable Image Conversion (TIC). While basic operations such as seasonal-
trend decomposition or RGB mapping are individually established, the technical novelty of Traceable
Image Conversion (TIC) lies in their synergistic integration into an informative, artifact-free 1D-to-2D
interface tailored for TSAD. Specifically, TIC operates through four core mechanisms:
      1. Semantic Channel Mapping: Allocating the raw series, trend, and residual components
         to separate RGB channels jointly exposes low-frequency structural deviations and high-
         frequency residual spikes to the visual backbone.
      2. High-Density Context Aggregation: Condensing a 5000-step time series into 196 visual
         tokens aggregates long-range context while exploiting pre-trained vision models, accelerat-
         ing inference ∼ 100× compared to plot-rendering baselines (e.g., 0.03s/iter vs. ViT4TS at
         3.14s/iter).
      3. Dimension-Aware Scaling: Decoupling scaling along the two axes respects their distinct
         physical semantics: temporal interpolation ensures continuity along the time axis, while
         copy-padding along the period axis prevents visual boundary artifacts.
      4. Traceable Patch Alignment: Constraining the folding width exactly to the ViT patch
         size (16) guarantees that each visual token encapsulates strictly continuous 1D timestamps,
         enabling precise 2D-to-1D feature alignment.

D.3        Comparison of multimodal fusion strategies

To justify feature-level temporal alignment, we evaluated late-fusion baselines as well as direct
feature fusion strategies, with detailed performance comparison reported in Table 15. Our Patch-level
Alignment outperforms them by resolving two critical limitations:

           1. Limitations of Late Fusion: Late fusion restricts the model to local optima and cannot
              leverage early cross-modal interaction. Consequently, visual global context cannot guide
              temporal features, and precise temporal features cannot rectify visual localization errors,
              inevitably leading to error accumulation.
           2. Limitations of Direct Feature Fusion: Visual patches inherently lack explicit temporal
              indices. Naively concatenating multimodal features introduces severe spatio-temporal noise.
              Our alignment anchors visual tokens to corresponding temporal tokens, which is crucial for
              point-wise TSAD localization.

Furthermore, our superiority stems from structurally indispensable cross-modal interactions that
resolve late-fusion’s architectural bottleneck.

D.4        Ablation study on training objectives

To analyze how different multi-task optimization objectives interact during joint training, we sys-
tematically evaluate combinations of Binary Cross-Entropy (LBCE ), Mean Squared Error (LM SE ),
Anomaly Window Contrastive Loss (Law ), and Routing Entropy Regularization (Le ). The ablation
study in Table 16 illustrates these objective interactions. Law (anomaly contrastive) generally works
cooperatively with LBCE (anomaly supervision) increasing separation between normal and anoma-
lous features. Conversely, the temporal fidelity regularized by LM SE conflicts with the sharp anomaly
boundaries required by LBCE . Crucially, Le helps reconcile these objectives via Task-Adaptive
Routing to achieve peak performance.

pairs is challenging on densely anomalous datasets, AWCL helps the model distinguish normal
from anomalous patterns more sharply. Specifically, AWCL is essential for capturing both fine-
grained local variations (e.g., pushing NAB VUS-PR from 31.23% in V4 to 32.98% in the full

Task-adaptive Multi-modal Fusion (TMF): Eliminating TMF (e.g., variant V3, which relies on
simple concatenation) causes consistent performance regression across benchmarks, such as a drop in
VUS-PR from 94.20% to 93.76% on TODS and from 88.61% to 85.31% on YAHOO. This validates
that the task-aware gating mechanism dynamically regulates feature integration, ensuring robust

Building on this component-wise validation, we also observe performance degradations on specific
datasets (MGAB, Stock, Power). Analyzing the variants reveals failure modes tied to intrinsic dataset
properties:

        • Non-Periodic Signals (MGAB, Stock): These datasets feature deterministic chaos (MGAB)
          or stochastic random walks (Stock) without meaningful periodicity. Forcing them into a 2D
          grid generates visual noise. Consequently, performance drops immediately upon introducing
          Traceable Image Conversion (V1), and downstream fusion cannot recover it.
        • Periodic Spike Anomalies (Power): In the Power dataset, normal sequences lack spikes,
          whereas anomalies manifest as periodic spike noise. Because our visual conversion (TIC)
          explicitly constructs 2D representations based on periodicity, it inadvertently encodes these
          periodic anomalies as normal background structures. While the temporal modality correctly
          identifies them (making V3 the best performer), the zero-shot fusion router (V4) incorrectly
          assigns high weight to this deceived visual representation, triggering massive negative
          transfer.

critical auxiliary constraint, promoting deep feature interaction and enhancing the robustness of fused
representations for the primary anomaly classification task.
